Accumulators: the operators' golden goose

Published on 29 July 2026 · Updated on 29 October 2026

The accumulator has everything going for it to appeal to recreational bettors: a few "safe" selections, odds that multiply, a spectacular potential payout for a small stake. What the interface does not show is that by multiplying the odds, you multiply something else too: the bookmakers' margins.

The mechanics

In an accumulator, the total odd is the product of the odds of each selection. But every odd carries its own margin. Multiply them, and the margins compound. The rule is simple and unforgiving:

RTP of the accumulator = RTP of selection 1 × RTP of selection 2 × …

Three selections taken on markets with a 95% RTP each: 0.95 × 0.95 × 0.95 = 85.7%. The margin has grown from 5% to 14.3%.

What it looks like as selections stack up

Assuming all selections are taken at a 95% RTP, a rather favourable level, typical of main markets:

577.4%22.6%
673.5%26.5%

866.3%33.7%

At five selections, more than a fifth of the stake goes to margin; at eight, a third. And if the selections come from side markets priced worse than 95%, those numbers degrade even faster.

Why accumulators are pushed so hard

It is therefore no accident that betting interfaces make accumulators easy, suggest them and dress them up with dedicated promotions: they are, structurally, the highest-margin bets in the catalogue, and the ones that earn them the most. "Acca boosts" never offset the enormous compounded margin these bets carry by nature for the bookmakers (see the table above).

In conclusion

None of this forbids accumulators: they offer a shot at a big payout for a small stake, and that asymmetry may be exactly what a bettor is looking for. But it has to be said plainly: the accumulator is, structurally, the type of bet that is most unfavourable to bettors over the long run.

That is why the RTP Calculator app deliberately leaves this format out. Nothing stops you, however, from calculating the RTP of each of your selections individually, then seeing for yourself how multiplying the margins degrades the overall RTP once those selections are bundled into a single accumulator.
