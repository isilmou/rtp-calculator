How to calculate a bookmaker's margin

Published on 29 July 2026

A bookmaker's margin is its commission — but unlike brokerage fees, it is written nowhere: it is hidden inside the odds. The good news is that it can be computed in three operations, with nothing more than a basic calculator.

The three-step method

Step 1 — convert each odd into an implied probability: probability = 1 ÷ decimal odd. Step 2 — add up the implied probabilities of every outcome of the market. Step 3 — derive the RTP and the margin: RTP = 100 ÷ total × 100, and margin = 100 − RTP.

Two-outcome example (tennis)

Player A at 1.80, player B at 1.95.

1÷1.80 = 55.6%; 1÷1.95 = 51.3%. Total: 106.8%.

RTP = 100 ÷ 106.8 × 100 = 93.6% → margin: 6.4%.

Three-outcome example (football, 1X2)

Home 2.10, draw 3.40, away 3.60.

47.6% + 29.4% + 27.8% = 104.8%.

RTP = 100 ÷ 104.8 × 100 = 95.4% → margin: 4.6%.

The method is identical whatever the number of outcomes: a 30-runner outright market is computed exactly the same way, by adding the 30 implied probabilities.

What makes the margin vary

Measure the margin on a handful of markets and patterns quickly emerge. The competition: the Premier League and other top leagues are priced tighter than lower divisions or exotic competitions, where the margin can be several times higher. The market type: in football, for example, the main 1X2 is usually the most competitive market of a match; side markets (goalscorers, corners, correct scores, handicaps) often carry substantially higher margins. Timing: far from kick-off, uncertainty is higher and margins wider; they tighten as the match approaches. The event: on some very big fixtures, operators temporarily compress their margin for commercial reasons.
