How to calculate the RTP of a sports bet and the bookmaker's margin

Published on 29 July 2026 · Updated on 24 October 2026

The RTP (Return To Player) of a sports bet is displayed nowhere: betting sites show odds, never the share of the stakes those odds give back. Yet this figure, and its complement the bookmaker's margin, can be computed from the odds themselves, in three operations that any basic calculator can handle.

The formula in three operations

1. Convert each odd into an implied probability: probability = 1 ÷ decimal odd. Odds of 2.00 are worth 50%, odds of 4.00 are worth 25%.

2. Add up the implied probabilities of every outcome of the market. If the odds were fair, the total would be exactly 100%; in practice, it always exceeds it.

3. Derive the RTP: RTP = 100 ÷ total × 100. The bookmaker's margin is what is missing to reach 100%: margin = 100 − RTP.

Two-outcome example (tennis)

Player A at 1.80, player B at 1.95.

1÷1.80 = 55.6%; 1÷1.95 = 51.3%. Total: 106.8%.

RTP = 100 ÷ 106.8 × 100 = 93.6%. Bookmaker's margin: 100 − 93.6 = 6.4%.

In other words, on this match, bettors share on average €93.60 for every €100 staked, whoever wins; the remaining €6.40 goes to the operator.

Three-outcome example (football, 1X2)

Home 2.10, draw 3.40, away 3.60.

47.6% + 29.4% + 27.8% = 104.8%.

RTP = 100 ÷ 104.8 × 100 = 95.4%. Margin: 4.6%.

The method does not change with the number of outcomes: a 30-runner outright market is computed the same way, by adding the 30 implied probabilities. The more outcomes a market has, the further the sum tends to drift from 100%, and the lower the RTP.

What if the odds are not decimal?

The formula assumes decimal (European) odds. Fractional or American odds are converted first: 6/4 becomes 2.50, +150 also becomes 2.50, −200 becomes 1.50. The guide on odds formats details these conversions. The format has no effect on the result: the margin hides just as well in a fraction as in a decimal.

What the result tells you

The RTP is the real price of the bet, and the margin is the operator's commission. Unlike brokerage fees, it is written nowhere: it is hidden in the odds, and no regulation requires displaying it market by market. Two odds of 1.85 on two different matches look equivalent; if the opposite odd is 1.85 in one case and 2.05 in the other, the first market has an RTP of 92.5% and the second 97.2%. That is exactly what the calculation makes visible.

What makes the RTP vary

A few calculations are enough for patterns to emerge. The competition: the Premier League and other top leagues show better RTPs than lower divisions or exotic competitions, where the margin can be several times higher. The market type: in football, the main 1X2 is usually the most competitive market of a match; side markets (goalscorers, corners, correct scores, handicaps) often carry substantially higher margins. Timing: far from kick-off, uncertainty is higher and margins wider; they tighten as the match approaches. The event: on some very big fixtures, operators temporarily compress their margin for commercial reasons. The RTP is therefore measured market by market, not operator by operator.
